# Monetisation research brief

Research date: 2026-10-18. Prices are in USD unless noted and were read from vendor pricing pages on that date. Items marked **(unverified)** come from secondary sources, conflict between sources, or are my own estimates.

canvist's core is released under the Unlicense, so no licence can be gated: anyone may fork, embed, or resell the editor, the `yrs` sync layer, and the headless Rust, Dart, and TypeScript document model. This brief looks at how other editor and collaboration vendors make money, what developers resent, and which patterns still work when the code itself cannot be sold.

---

## 1. TipTap: the closest comparable

### Open-core split

- **MIT and free:** the editor, the extension ecosystem, and Hocuspocus, the Yjs WebSocket server. Self-hosted collaboration through Hocuspocus is free ([OSS commitment](https://tiptap.dev/open-source-to-platform), [Hocuspocus repo](https://github.com/ueberdosis/hocuspocus)).
- **Pro extensions that moved to MIT:** On 2025-06-06 TipTap moved Details, Emoji, DragHandle, FileHandler, InvisibleCharacters, Mathematics, TableOfContents, and UniqueID to MIT. It also said these extensions "are no longer at the center" of its business ([release note](https://tiptap.dev/blog/release-notes/were-open-sourcing-more-of-tiptap)).
- **What stays paid:** the "Tiptap Platform". It covers AI, comments, document history, version comparison, conversion, page layout, tracked changes, authentication, high availability, and SLA support ([OSS commitment](https://tiptap.dev/open-source-to-platform)).

### Gating mechanism

Pro packages live under the `@tiptap-pro` scope on a private registry at `https://registry.tiptap.dev/`. Installing them needs a TipTap account and a personal npm token, which the docs suggest exposing as `TIPTAP_PRO_TOKEN` in `.npmrc`. Snapshots, Comments, and some AI Toolkit features also check for an active subscription ([Pro extensions guide](https://tiptap.dev/docs/guides/pro-extensions)). Both AI Toolkit packages, the server toolkit and the browser client toolkit, are "paid add-ons to your subscription", sold through sales. The toolkit works with any LLM the customer chooses ([AI Toolkit overview](https://tiptap.dev/docs/content-ai/capabilities/ai-toolkit/overview)).

### Pricing model (June 2025 to present)

On 2025-06-06 TipTap dropped its old mix of concurrent connections, monthly connections, and feature tiers. It now bills on two things: which paid features a customer uses, and how many documents are stored in TipTap Cloud. It also removed the free Cloud plan and replaced it with a trial ([new pricing model](https://tiptap.dev/blog/release-notes/tiptaps-new-pricing-model-is-live)). Current tiers ([pricing](https://tiptap.dev/pricing), [pricing.md](https://tiptap.dev/pricing.md)):

| ---------- | ------- | ------------------ | -------------------------- | ------------ | ------------ |
| Start      | $59     | $49                | 500                        | 2            | 2            |

- **Start:** collaboration, inline AI, DOCX and Markdown import, export to DOCX, PDF, ODT, EPUB, and Markdown, document history, comments, UI components, and a choice of US or EU hosting.
- **Team adds:** version comparison, page-based layouts ("Pages"), webhooks and REST APIs, backend document manipulation (the successor to the old Document Server), and email support.
- **Business adds:** priority support, a 99.9% uptime guarantee, and a temporary Slack channel.
- **Enterprise adds:** on-premises deployment, your own auth, storage, or AI models, SOC 2 Type II, LTS, and custom development.
- **Sold through sales on any plan:** AI Toolkit and Tracked Changes.
- **Other add-ons:** extra developer licences at $49/month, or $39/month billed annually. Document packs raise the per-environment cap to 50k (Start), 200k (Team), or 500k (Business) ([pricing](https://tiptap.dev/pricing)).
- **Not published:** connection limits, AI credits, and conversion quotas. AI usage appears to be either bring-your-own-model or negotiated (unverified).
- **What counts as a document:** only documents stored in TipTap Cloud. Documents in your own database do not count, and deleting a document frees its slot.
- **Overage:** TipTap does not block new documents. It sends an email, and "if you don't respond" upgrades the plan automatically. It offers no refunds. Open-source, education, and startup discounts are "case by case" ([pricing.md](https://tiptap.dev/pricing.md)).
- **Trial scope:** The plan table limits the 30-day trial to Start and Team, but the FAQ says every plan gets it **(unverified which is current)**.
- **Pricing page structure:** an open-source reassurance first, then a plan table with document, licence, and environment rows, per-plan highlights, a section of add-ons sold through sales, and an FAQ on document counting, trials, refunds, SOC 2, hosting, and discounts ([pricing](https://tiptap.dev/pricing)).

## 3. What developers dislike and what earns goodwill

### Dislikes

1. **Relicensing a permissive editor.** TinyMCE 7 moved from MIT to GPL 2+ on 2024-03-20. Downstream projects such as BookStack refused to impose the change on their users. Commenters said self-hosting was treated as "a second-class or 'only for enterprises' citizen", and that usage pricing is unbounded risk for free software. Maintainers closed the discussion in September 2024 ([GitHub #9496](https://github.com/tinymce/tinymce/discussions/9496)). The community forked the last MIT release as HugeRTE ([README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/hugerte@1.0.12/README.md)).
2. **Mandatory licence keys, even for GPL users.** CKEditor v44 made `licenseKey` required, so GPL users must pass `'GPL'` ([v44 update guide](https://ckeditor.com/docs/ckeditor5/latest/updating/guides/update-to-44.html)). Drupal sites broke with `license-key-missing` until the integration defaulted the key ([Drupal issue](https://www.drupal.org/i/3491464)). HN commenters have called CKEditor's licensing unclear and too expensive for early startups ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=28256557)).
3. **Fear that an MIT editor is heading for a paywall.** When TipTap changed its wording from "MIT" to "free" in 2023, one HN commenter wrote: "This makes me extremely worried" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=36960818)). TipTap staff had to promise the core stays MIT ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=36976791)).
4. **Bundles that force a tier jump.** In 2025 one developer complained that the $149 Team plan was the only way to get the AI frontend components, with no cheaper option without collaboration ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44190855)). TipTap staff had already admitted that some Pro extensions "don't justify the price of the whole tier" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=36992359)). A 2022 GitHub thread asked for a lifetime licence instead of €29/month for three extensions ([#2653](https://github.com/ueberdosis/tiptap/discussions/2653)).
5. **Per-connection and per-visitor charges.** One HN user was put off by Liveblocks "charging $0.01 for every time a client connects" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=32511027)). Liveblocks later named billing for one-time visitors as a top customer complaint ([blog](https://liveblocks.io/blog/introducing-fairer-billing-with-first-day-free)).
6. **Unclear boundaries between free and paid.** A December 2025 question asking exactly what self-hosted Hocuspocus permits commercially got no answer ([GitHub #7321](https://github.com/ueberdosis/tiptap/discussions/7321)). Third-party guides describe developers building on the free editor and only later finding that collaboration and AI need the paid platform ([DEV](https://dev.to/eddyter/tiptap-pricing-explained-2026-free-vs-pro-vs-cloud-cost-breakdown-3l5g)).
7. **Hosted scripts that cannot be pinned.** TinyMCE's cloud channels auto-update, so Subresource Integrity is impossible. Users raised this after the Polyfill.io incident ([GitHub #9496](https://github.com/tinymce/tinymce/discussions/9496)).
8. **Removing free tiers, refusing refunds, upgrading automatically.** TipTap sunset its free Cloud tier ([release note](https://tiptap.dev/blog/release-notes/were-open-sourcing-more-of-tiptap)), offers no refunds, and upgrades plans automatically when customers exceed limits ([pricing.md](https://tiptap.dev/pricing.md)).

### Goodwill

- **Open-sourcing formerly paid code.** TipTap's 2022 and 2025 moves closed the GitHub complaint threads ([#2653](https://github.com/ueberdosis/tiptap/discussions/2653), [release note](https://tiptap.dev/blog/release-notes/were-open-sourcing-more-of-tiptap)).
- **Charging for operations, not code.** One HN commenter said they had "no problem paying" for managed services such as Liveblocks ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=36981460)). An earlier commenter praised TipTap's "sensible licensing model" compared with TinyMCE and CKEditor ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=36958190)).
- **Fair-use billing details.** Liveblocks does not bill one-time visitors ([blog](https://liveblocks.io/blog/introducing-fairer-billing-with-first-day-free)). TipTap counts only documents stored in its cloud and never blocks document creation ([pricing.md](https://tiptap.dev/pricing.md)). Velt bills only for documents with CRUD activity ([pricing](https://velt.dev/pricing)).
- **Flat, predictable licences.** Froala advertises no usage limits, no seat fees, and a lifetime option ([cart](https://cart.froala.com/)). Plate Plus sells one-time, per-team source access ([Plate Plus](https://pro.platejs.org/)).
- **Hard caps instead of surprise bills on free tiers.** Liveblocks pauses a feature at its cap until the next cycle ([pricing](https://liveblocks.io/pricing)).

## 4. Metering and billing for Railway-hosted Rust services

**Railway costs.** Pro is $20/month including $20 of usage. Resources cost $10 per GB of RAM per month, $20 per vCPU per month, $0.05 per GB of egress, and $0.15 per GB-month of volume storage ([plans](https://docs.railway.com/reference/pricing/plans)). Railway Serverless sleeps a service after 5 to 10 minutes without outbound traffic ([app sleeping](https://docs.railway.com/reference/app-sleeping)). An open sync relay will probably stay awake, and idle-but-open WebSockets have not been tested **(unverified)**. For sync, RAM is the main cost driver because `yrs` documents stay in memory. Price on active documents and storage, not on messages.

**Options:**

- **Stripe Billing Meters.** This is the simplest option. Stripe Billing costs 0.7% of billing volume on pay-as-you-go, and Meters include up to 100M events/month ([Stripe Billing pricing, UK page](https://stripe.com/billing/pricing); the US rate is assumed to be the same, **unverified**). Stripe now calls Metronome, which it owns, its "primary usage-based billing platform" for new integrations, but still fully supports Meters and integrates them better with Checkout ([usage-based docs](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/usage-based)). Meters are enough for a few meters with tiered prices.
- **Lago.** AGPL-3.0 and self-hostable with Docker Compose. Ingestion is idempotent by `transaction_id`, and batches are atomic ([repo](https://github.com/getlago/lago)). Credits, dunning, and the customer portal require Premium ([pricing](https://getlago.com/pricing)). It is a heavy extra service to run on Railway.
- **OpenMeter.** Apache-2.0. Kong acquired it on 2025-09-03, and its cloud is moving into Kong Konnect ([Kong](https://konghq.com/blog/news/kong-acquires-openmeter), [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/09/03/kong-acquires-openmeter-power-api-ai-monetization/)). It is a good fit for raw event metering, but its direction now follows Kong's.
- **Orb.** Adyen agreed to buy it for $335M, with closing around 2026-07-01 ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/partnerships/2026/adyen-aims-to-tackle-complex-ai-pricing-with-orb-acquisition/)). Pricing is custom and enterprise-only ([Stigg](https://www.stigg.io/blog-posts/orb-pricing)), which is overkill at launch.

**Recommended pattern.** Have the Rust sync service write usage rows to Postgres, such as `(org, environment, doc_id, day)` for each active document, plus storage bytes and conversion counts. Run an hourly job that sends per-customer aggregates to Stripe Meters with an idempotency identifier. Postgres stays the source of truth for the in-product usage dashboard, and Stripe only invoices. Moving to Metronome, Lago, or OpenMeter later only changes the export step.

## Recommendations for canvist

### Copy

- **Monetise operations, not code.** Make everything client-side and the reference server Unlicense, and say so loudly, for example "self-host the exact binary we run". Sell the hosted service, which is uptime, storage, scaling, history, conversion, and compliance, as Liveblocks and TipTap Cloud do. This also solves TipTap's stated problem that extensions "are no longer at the center" of its business.
- **Bill on stored or active documents.** TipTap uses stored documents and Velt uses monthly active documents. This unit tracks canvist's real cost, which is RAM per loaded `yrs` doc, and ignores how many viewers or which platform connects. Flutter, React Native, Lynx, and native apps all count the same.
- **Free dev and staging environments,** per TipTap's environment model.
- **Hard caps on free plans, warnings on paid plans,** and no automatic upgrades.
- **A pricing page structured like TipTap's,** with "the editor is free forever" at the top. Add a usage calculator.
- **Narrow, one-time UI kits,** like Plate Plus. A canvas editor needs polished toolbars, menus, and comment UI for each platform, and a template pack is cheap to sell without touching the core's licence.

### Avoid

- **Licence keys, watermarks, private npm registries, and GPL or XL splits.** The Unlicense makes them unenforceable, and the CKEditor and TinyMCE episodes show they burn goodwill. Never require a key to render the editor.
- **Billing on editor loads or connections.** These punish exactly the multi-platform, multi-viewer use that makes canvist distinctive, and they are hard to count offline or in native apps.
- **Read-only editors on overage.** CKEditor Free does this. Degrade the hosted features instead, never the client.
- **Forcing a jump to a $150+ tier for one feature.** Sell AI and conversion as metered add-ons available on any paid plan.
- **Cloud-only scripts without SRI.** Ship versioned packages only.

### Product lineup

1. **canvist Sync:** managed `yrs` relay with persistence, JWT auth, presence, and webhooks, running on Railway in US and EU regions.
2. **canvist History:** snapshots, named versions, and diff and restore through the headless model.
3. **canvist Convert:** a server-side REST API from canvist JSON to and from Markdown, HTML, and plain text first, then DOCX and PDF. It runs on the headless Rust model.
4. **canvist Review:** comments, mentions, notifications, and tracked changes. Build this later.
5. **canvist AI:** document-aware edits through the headless model. Bring your own key for free, or use managed tokens billed at cost plus 20%.
6. **Self-host Support and LTS:** a support subscription for teams that run the open server themselves.
7. **UI Kits:** a one-time purchase of polished per-platform chrome.

**Margin check (my estimate, unverified).** Assume an average in-memory `yrs` document of about 200 KB and that around 10% of MADs are loaded concurrently. Then 150k MADs need roughly 3 GB of RAM, about $30/month at Railway rates, plus storage and egress. Unit prices therefore carry a healthy margin even at the Scale overage rate. Benchmark real document sizes before publishing prices.

**Discounts.** Make the open-source and early-stage discounts public, for example free Builder for OSI-licensed projects and 50% off for the first year for companies under five people. Don't leave them "case by case".

### Build first

1. **Sync with MAD metering and Stripe Meters.** This is the highest value per unit of effort. canvist already has first-class `yrs`. A Rust relay with Postgres or S3 persistence, JWT auth, and a usage table is a small service. To compete with the free Hocuspocus Railway template, it needs a one-click setup, a dashboard, metering, and multi-region support.
2. **Convert to Markdown, HTML, and JSON.** Almost free to build, because the headless Rust model already exists. It also makes the server useful to AI agents and backends, not only to editors. DOCX and PDF come next, since TipTap, CKEditor, and TinyMCE all charge per conversion, which shows the demand.
3. **History and snapshots.** Snapshots come cheaply from `yrs` state vectors, and history is the first feature teams ask for after sync.
4. **The pricing page with a calculator and a published self-host guide.** Lead with trust: the editor is free forever.
5. **Later:** the Review suite, managed AI, UI Kits, SOC 2, and an Enterprise tier, once paying Scale customers ask for them.
